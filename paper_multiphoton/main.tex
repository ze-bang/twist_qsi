\title{Saturation of the Emergent Electric Field Makes the
Photon Anharmonic at Lattice Momenta}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The emergent photon of quantum spin ice is described at long wavelength
by Gaussian lattice electrodynamics, a harmonic theory whose two
parameters enter the transverse spectrum only through a common
prefactor. That rigidity fixes three statements at every momentum and
for every choice of parameters: the single-mode state
$S^z(\bq)|0\rangle$ is exactly an eigenstate, so Feynman's ratio
saturates at $f_1/S=\omega$; each transverse channel carries a single
spectral pole; and the two transverse polarizations are degenerate, which
we verify by diagonalizing the lattice curl at $153$ allowed momenta of
three tori. Compactness must break all three, because the emergent
electric field is saturated at $S^z=\pm\frac12$: a field wave cannot be
built by raising an amplitude, only by rearranging which links carry
which of the two allowed values, and exponentially many ice
configurations carry the same wave. We compute the operator-resolved
$\Szz(\bq,\omega)$ on $48$-, $64$-, $72$- and $96$-site clusters, the
largest a constrained sector of dimension $1.47\times10^{8}$, and find
all three identities violated by order one and all three most strongly at
the zone-boundary $L$ point, where the variational bound overshoots by
$1.68$, a third of the channel weight leaves the dominant pole, and the
doublet splits by $1.69$. The bound overshoot ranks the five resolved
momenta exactly as the softening does. The mechanism is exact and
parameter-free: in the ice basis the probe is diagonal, so a ring
exchange either shifts the density-wave amplitude by
$a(c')-a(c)=\pm g_p$ or leaves it untouched, and the hexagons that leave
it untouched are those lying in the wavefronts---one family in four when
$\bq$ is parallel to a $\langle111\rangle$ axis. Flipping those costs no
spectral weight, and the soft eigenstate exploits it, recovering $89\%$
of the dephasing the rigid wave suffers on the driven plaquettes and
paying $0.108K$ there against its partner's $1.082K$. A counterfactual
that removes the cancellation makes $L$ an ordinary momentum; at $X$,
where no family is dark, nothing softens. The result is not a new
excitation but the photon itself reorganized, and it predicts a
two-feature zone-boundary response whose $0.55$--$0.59$ energy ratio and
two-to-one weight ratio are independent of the overall energy scale.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\emph{Introduction.}---Quantum spin ice realizes a compact $U(1)$ lattice
gauge theory in a magnetic insulator. In the two-in--two-out manifold of
the pyrochlore lattice, virtual exchange resonates the six spins around
an elementary hexagon, and the deconfined phase supports a gapless
emergent photon together with gapped electric and magnetic
charges~\cite{Hermele2004,Castelnovo2008,Gingras2014,SavaryBalents2017,
RauGingras2019,Broholm2020}. At long wavelength this photon is described
by Gaussian lattice electrodynamics, a theory of decoupled harmonic
oscillators, and that description is quantitatively established and
underpins the search for candidate
materials~\cite{Benton2012,Shannon2012,KatoOnoda2015,Ross2011,
Sibille2018,Gaudet2019,Gao2019,Smith2022,Poree2025,Gao2025}.

Being harmonic makes the Gaussian theory unusually rigid, and that
rigidity is what we exploit. It has two free parameters, an electric
stiffness $U$ and the ring coupling $K$, and they enter the transverse
spectrum only through a common prefactor,
$\omega_\lambda(\bq)=\sqrt{2UK}\,\sigma_\lambda(\bq)$. Consequently three
statements hold at every momentum for \emph{every} choice of those
parameters: the single-mode state $S^z(\bq)|0\rangle$ is exactly an
eigenstate, so Feynman's ratio saturates, $f_1/S=\omega$; each transverse
channel has a single spectral pole; and the two transverse polarizations
are degenerate. No renormalization of a harmonic theory can violate any
of them. They are therefore a parameter-free baseline against which
departures can be measured without a fit.

Compactness must violate all three, and for a reason that is structural
rather than perturbative. The emergent electric field is microscopically
saturated, $E_\ell=S^z_\ell=\pm\frac12$. A field wave at wavevector $\bq$
therefore cannot be built by increasing an amplitude---there is no
amplitude to increase---but only by rearranging which links carry which
of the two allowed values. That rearrangement is not unique:
exponentially many ice configurations share the same density-wave
amplitude, so the excitation retains internal freedom at fixed spectral
weight. A harmonic theory has no such freedom, which is precisely why its
single-mode state is already exact. What is not obvious, and what we
compute, is whether the excitation uses that freedom, by how much, and
where.

We find that it does, dramatically, at zone-boundary momenta. Our results
are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item All three harmonic identities fail by order one, and all three fail
hardest at the zone-boundary $L$ point: the Feynman bound overshoots the
true level by $1.68$, a third of the channel weight leaves the dominant
pole, and the transverse doublet splits by $1.69$.
\item The departure from harmonicity is not scattered. Ranked across the
five momenta we resolve, the bound overshoot orders them \emph{exactly} as
the softening does, without inversion.
\item The mechanism is exact and requires no fitted parameter. In the ice
basis the probe is diagonal, so a ring exchange either shifts the
density-wave amplitude or does not; the hexagons that do not are those
lying in the wavefronts, one family in four when $\bq$ is parallel to a
$\langle111\rangle$ axis. Flipping them is motion at constant probe
value, hence free of spectral-weight cost.
\item The soft eigenstate exploits exactly this. It recovers $89\%$ of the
dephasing that the rigid wave suffers on the driven plaquettes, paying
only $0.108K$ there against its partner's $1.082K$---a factor of ten,
against an overall energy ratio of $0.59$.
\item The effect is momentum-specific and size-stable: removing the
geometric cancellation in a counterfactual makes $L$ an ordinary
momentum, no family is dark at $X$ and nothing softens there, and the
zone-boundary branch turns over on all three clusters we can
diagonalize.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Model, observable, method.}---We study the standard ring
Hamiltonian on the spin-ice manifold~\cite{Hermele2004,Shannon2012},
$H=-K\sum_p(W_p+W_p^\dagger)$ with
$W_p=S^+_1S^-_2S^+_3S^-_4S^+_5S^-_6$ over contractible elementary
hexagons; in gauge language $W_p=e^{iB_p}$ and $H$ is the compact
magnetic energy $-2K\sum_p\cos B_p$. It has no diagonal part, a fact we
use repeatedly. Since $S^z$ is the emergent electric field the natural
probe is
$\Szz(\bq,\omega)=N^{-1}\sum_{\mu,n}|\langle
n|A^\mu_{\bq}|0\rangle|^2\delta(\omega-\omega_n)$, with
$A^\mu_{\bq}=\sum_{i\in\mu}e^{-i\bq\cdot\bm r_i}S^z_i$ resolved by
sublattice $\mu$. All spectral fractions are normalized within $\Szz$ at
fixed $\bq$. This is the theoretical pseudospin response, not a
material-specific neutron cross section; the Supplemental Material
treats the difference in full.

We evaluate $\Szz$ by complete diagonalization at $48$ sites and by
Lanczos continued fractions at $64$, $72$ and $96$, the last in a winding
sector of dimension $146\,758\,352$. Sectors are certified exhaustively
at $48$ and $72$ sites and by the twelve largest of $519$ at $96$;
Krylov dimension $200$ against $120$ leaves the dominant poles unchanged
to machine precision. Details and convergence are in the Supplemental
Material.

\emph{The harmonic baseline is rigid.}---The gauge field lives on the
links of the diamond lattice, equivalently on the pyrochlore sites, four
per cubic cell, while the gradient that generates gauge transformations
has one column per diamond site, two per cell. Gauge invariance
$Cd_0=0$---which we verify to $0.0\times10^{0}$---therefore removes two
of the four link modes and leaves exactly two transverse polarizations,
the same count as Maxwell in three dimensions and for the same reason.

Their degeneracy is a separate fact and does not follow from that
counting. We verified it directly by diagonalizing the lattice curl at
every allowed momentum of fcc$(4,4,4)$, fcc$(3,4,5)$ and fcc$(2,3,5)$:
at all $153$ momenta, including $32$ of generic symmetry on the
anisotropic boxes, $\sigma_2/\sigma_1=1.00000000$, with
$\sigma_3/\sigma_1\sim10^{-15}$ confirming rank two. Because $U$ and $K$
multiply both members alike, no renormalization of the Gaussian theory
can split the doublet at all. Together with the two harmonic identities
noted above, this fixes the baseline $(H_1,H_2,H_3)=(1,0,1)$ of
Table~\ref{tab:harm} at every momentum and for every parameter choice.

one, all are largest at $L$, and the first---the overshoot of the

from being an eigenstate---orders the five momenta exactly as the

Two of these deserve emphasis. That $H_2=0.335$ means a third of the
weight in that channel has left its dominant pole: the response is not a
single renormalized quasiparticle even qualitatively. And $H_3=1.691$ is
a splitting of a doublet that the Gaussian theory holds exactly
degenerate for any parameters, so it is not a large correction within
that description but a failure of it. That interactions split these
polarizations at intermediate wavevector was predicted semiclassically by
Kwasigroch, Dou\c{c}ot and Castelnovo~\cite{Kwasigroch2017}; what is new
here is its size at the zone boundary, that only one member softens, and
the microscopic reason.

\emph{Why compactness permits this.}---The mechanism follows from one
observation. In the basis of ice configurations $|c\rangle$ the probe is
diagonal,
$A^\mu_{\bq}|c\rangle=a^\mu(c)|c\rangle$, whereas the ring exchange is
purely off-diagonal, taking $c$ to $c'$ with hexagon $p$ flipped. The
commutator $[A^\mu_{\bq},W_p]=g^\mu_p(\bq)W_p$ is therefore a statement
about configurations,
\begin{equation}
a^\mu(c')-a^\mu(c)=\pm\,g^\mu_p(\bq),
\qquad
g^\mu_p=\!\!\!\sum_{j\in p\cap\mu}\!\!\!(-1)^j e^{-i\bq\cdot\bm r_j},
\label{eq:shift}
\end{equation}
so $g^\mu_p$ is exactly the amount by which flipping $p$ shifts the
density-wave amplitude. We verified Eq.~(\ref{eq:shift}) on $4000$
configurations, every hexagon and every sublattice: the largest
discrepancy is $0.0\times10^{0}$.

This turns a dynamical question into a geometric one. A pyrochlore
hexagon is planar with its plane perpendicular to one of the four
$\langle111\rangle$ axes, and each hexagon omits one sublattice, so the
hexagons fall into four families. A plane wave is constant on planes
perpendicular to $\bq$; if a family's plane \emph{is} such a wavefront,
all six of its sites share a phase, the alternating sum cancels term by
term, and $g^\mu_p=0$ for every $\mu$. At $L$, where
$\bq\parallel[1\bar11]$, exactly one family satisfies this. Its measured
geometric weight is $0.0000$ against $96.0000$ for each of the other
three, and its axis is parallel to $\bq$ to six digits,
$|\hat{\bm n}\cdot\hat{\bq}|=1.000000$. We call it \emph{dark}.

Now the consequence. Because $H$ has no diagonal part, energy is ring
coherence and nothing else, and every excitation carries an exact
additive bill,
$\omega_n=K\sum_p[\langle0|W_p+W_p^\dagger|0\rangle-\langle
n|W_p+W_p^\dagger|n\rangle]$. Writing the excitation as the ground state
modulated, $\psi(c)\simeq a(c)\psi_0(c)$, its coherence on $p$ is a sum
over the connected pairs of $a^*(c')a(c)\psi_0(c')\psi_0(c)$. Since
$\psi_0>0$, the ground-state version of that sum interferes
constructively---that is what makes the ground state cheap. In the
excited state, by Eq.~(\ref{eq:shift}), a dark plaquette has
$a(c')=a(c)$ and the factor is $|a|^2>0$: the pair still resonates in
phase and \emph{no} coherence is lost. A bright plaquette has
$a(c')\neq a(c)$, the pair dephases, and the lost coherence is the
energy. This is also what the $f$-sum rule has been saying:
$f_1=\frac{K}{2N}\sum_p\langle W_p+W_p^\dagger\rangle\sum_\mu|g^\mu_p|^2$
is a sum of dephasings, and it is blind to the dark family because a
dark plaquette cannot be dephased.

\emph{The state uses the freedom.}---Availability is a property of the
momentum; the dark family is equally available to every level at $L$. What
distinguishes the eigenstates is what they do with it, and the exact
ledger settles that (Fig.~\ref{fig:ledger}a). The rigid single-mode
state costs $1.846K$, of which $0.968K$ is dephasing on the bright
families. The soft eigenstate costs $1.024K$, of which only $0.108K$ is
bright: it has recovered $89\%$ of the dephasing while leaving its
dark-family cost essentially unchanged, so the entire relaxation of
$0.822K$ is bright-family recovery ($105\%$ of it, the dark contribution
being $-5\%$). Its partner at the same momentum recovers nothing and
pays $1.082K$ there, a factor of ten more, against an overall energy
ratio of $0.59$. The same pattern holds at $72$ sites, where the
bright-family ratio is $0.282$ against an overall $0.589$.

The soft mode is therefore a density wave dressed so as to re-phase the
resonances the wave would otherwise dephase, depositing what remains on
the family where flipping does not change the probe value and so costs no
spectral weight. It buys energy in a currency the probe cannot see---and
saturation of $E$ is what makes that currency exist.

\emph{Controls.}---Three checks establish that this is the operative
mechanism rather than a description of it. At $X$ no $\langle111\rangle$
axis is parallel to $\bq$, the geometric weights are $(96,96,96,96)$, no
family is dark: there is nowhere to deposit the disturbance and nothing
softens, the lowest level sitting at $0.952$ of its Gaussian energy
against $0.503$ at $L$. In a counterfactual in which the dark family is
given the mean bright weight, $f_1(L)$ rises from $0.6835$ to $0.8797$
---which coincides with $f_1(X)=0.8797$, confirming that the entire
suppression at $L$ is the dark family---and $L$'s predictor becomes
$1.168$, tied with $(\frac13,\frac13,\frac23)$ at $1.177$, a momentum
that softens only to $0.824$. Remove the cancellation and $L$ stops being
special. Finally the ground state anticipates all of it: $S(L)=0.3704$ is
the largest structure factor of any resolved momentum, and $f_1/S$
normalized by the Gaussian energy ranks the five momenta exactly as the
measured softening does, a prediction available before any excited state
is computed and carried to $2048$ spins by sign-free Green's-function
Monte Carlo, where the $L$-centered minimum persists with $1.7\%$ drift
(Fig.~\ref{fig:thermo}).

\emph{Consequences and robustness.}---The mechanism bends the
dispersion. Walking outward along $\Gamma\to L$ the branch first rises
like a photon---at $t=\frac13$ it lies \emph{above} the Gaussian curve
and is nearly degenerate with its partner, $1.895$ against
$1.980$---then turns over and falls into the zone boundary, by $46\%$,
$42\%$ and $31\%$ at $48$, $64$ and $72$ sites, against the partner's
$12.6\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:dispersion}). Each cluster resolves one interior
momentum on that line, so what is established is the inequality
$\omega(\text{interior})>\omega(L)$ at three independent sizes rather
than the shape of a minimum.

The scale-free measures are the stable ones
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder}, Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}): the branch ratio is
$0.55$--$0.59$ across a factor $1.6\times10^{4}$ in sector dimension and
three cluster shapes, and the soft branch holds $50$--$54\%$ of the
response throughout. Absolute energies drift by about ten percent, as
expected at these sizes. Not everything is stable and we say which: the
polarization splitting at $X$ falls from $42\%$ at $48$ sites to $6\%$ at
$64$, and we draw no conclusion from it.

\emph{Relation to the roton.}---The phenomenology is Feynman's, and it is
worth saying exactly how far the parallel runs. Our $f_1/S$ is literally
his construction; $S(\bq)$ peaks where the mode softens, as it does at the
helium roton; his bound overshoots by $1.80$ here against roughly a
factor two in helium, and in both cases relaxation below it is the
physics. The dispersion turns over. And, as with the helium roton, this
is not a new quasiparticle: it is one of the two transverse
polarizations, continuously connected to the photon doublet at small
$\bq$, carrying no new quantum number---phonon and roton are likewise one
branch.

The parallel stops at the mechanism. In helium Galilean invariance fixes
$f_1=\hbar^2k^2/2m$, so the numerator carries no structure and a roton
minimum can only come from $S(k)$ peaking. Here both factors move: against
$X$, $S$ is enhanced by $36\%$---helium's mechanism---while $f_1$ is
suppressed by $22\%$ through an exact lattice cancellation that has no
continuum counterpart, and the counterfactual above shows the second is
what makes $L$ the minimum. Roughly half of the effect is a mechanism a
liquid cannot have. We therefore use ``roton-like'' descriptively and
rest no claim on it.

\emph{Experimental implications.}---The signature is a two-feature
zone-boundary response where a renormalized photon would give one.
Following $[111]$ outward, a single dispersing mode at small $\bq$
separates into two, the splitting growing from unresolvable to a factor
$1.7$ at the zone boundary, with the \emph{lower} feature carrying two to
three times the weight of the upper. Two aspects are scale-free and so
testable without knowing $K$: the energy ratio, $0.55$--$0.59$, and the
weight ratio. A renormalized photon velocity produces neither, and no
Gaussian theory produces a splitting at all. Because the effect tracks
$f_1/S$ rather than the label $L$, the same signature should appear along
$(t,\frac12,\frac12)$, as it does in our $72$-site spectrum.

Two caveats bound this. A material-specific cross section reweights the
sublattice amplitudes coherently, so the theoretical $50$--$54\%$
fraction is not a universal neutron intensity; and longer ring exchanges
and virtual spinons can modify the zone-boundary spectrum of a real
material. The robust prediction of the minimal model is the
redistribution itself.

\emph{Discussion.}---Harmonic lattice electrodynamics fails at the
lattice scale in quantum spin ice, and it fails by order one in three
independent, parameter-free respects at once. The reason is that the
emergent electric field is saturated. A harmonic theory builds a field
wave by raising an amplitude, and the resulting single-mode state is
already an eigenstate with nothing left to optimize. A compact theory has
no amplitude to raise and must instead choose among exponentially many
configurations carrying the same wave---and it chooses well, re-phasing
the ring resonances the wave would dephase and depositing what remains
where the probe is blind. The lattice decides where that is possible: at
$\bq$ parallel to a $\langle111\rangle$ axis, one hexagon family in four
lies in the wavefronts and drops out of the oscillator strength entirely.

What this is not is a new excitation. It is the emergent photon,
reorganized where compactness and geometry together allow it. The open
questions are whether the reorganization survives virtual spinons and
longer ring exchanges in microscopic XXZ models, and whether the
finite-size pole remains sharp in the thermodynamic limit---which
requires dynamical rather than ground-state Monte Carlo, since beyond
$96$ spins we constrain the bound and not the pole.
