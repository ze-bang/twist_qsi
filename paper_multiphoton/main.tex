\begin{abstract}
The emergent photon of quantum spin ice is well understood at long
wavelength, but at lattice momenta the compact $U(1)$ theory has no
small parameter---the electric field is saturated at
$S^z=\pm\frac12$, so a field wave cannot be reduced in amplitude, only
rearranged---and which eigenstates carry the spin response there has
never been resolved. We compute the operator-resolved
$\Szz(\bq,\omega)$ of the pure pyrochlore ring-exchange model on 48-,
64-, 72-, and 96-site clusters, the largest a constrained sector of
dimension $1.47\times10^{8}$. In the Gaussian theory the two
transverse modes are exactly degenerate at every momentum, and no
renormalization of its parameters can split them; we measure splittings
up to a factor $1.7$ that are momentum-specific rather than functions
of $|\bq|$---two momenta at identical $|\bq|$ split by $1.69$ and
$1.06$---and are largest at the zone-boundary $L$ point, where the
lower member collapses to half the Gaussian energy and takes $50$--$54\%$ of the longitudinal weight,
against $0.82$--$1.11$ of that energy at every other momentum
resolved. The dominant zone-boundary response is therefore not a
photon. That mode is elementary---the lowest excitation of its
sector---and its photon-like partner is excluded from being a two-mode
composite by spin-reversal parity and by kinematics independently. Its
softness is predicted before any excited state is computed by the
Feynman ratio $f_1(\bq)/S(\bq)$, a rigorous variational bound built
from ground-state data, which ranks all five resolved momenta in
exactly the order of their measured softening; its two factors
identify the mechanism as an exact curl cancellation on one hexagon
family multiplied by a quantum enhancement of the static correlations
that is absent in the classical ice manifold. The reconstruction is
stable across four clusters, three cluster shapes, and a factor
$1.6\times10^{4}$ in Hilbert-space dimension, and sign-free
Green's-function Monte Carlo carries the ground-state diagnostic to
$2048$ spins with its minimum still at $L$. The phenomenology is
Feynman's and the object is a lattice roton, predicting a two-feature
zone-boundary response whose energy ratio $0.55$--$0.59$ and
two-to-one weight ratio are both independent of the overall energy
scale.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle
\emph{Introduction.}---Quantum spin ice realizes a compact $U(1)$
lattice gauge theory in a magnetic insulator. In the two-in--two-out
manifold of the pyrochlore lattice, virtual exchange resonates the six
spins around an elementary hexagon, and the resulting deconfined phase
supports a gapless emergent photon together with gapped electric and
magnetic charges~\cite{Hermele2004,Castelnovo2008,Gingras2014,
SavaryBalents2017,RauGingras2019,Broholm2020}. That long-wavelength
description is quantitatively established and motivates the search for
candidate materials~\cite{Benton2012,Shannon2012,KatoOnoda2015,
Ross2011,Sibille2018,Gaudet2019,Gao2019,Smith2022,Poree2025,Gao2025}.

At lattice momenta the same theory is a different problem, and the
reason is structural. The emergent electric field is microscopically
saturated, $E_\ell=S^z_\ell=\pm\frac12$, so $\sum_\ell E_\ell^2$ is a
constant inside the constrained manifold: there is no electric
stiffness to tune, no coupling constant to send to zero, and hence no
free-field limit from which the interacting theory can be reached
perturbatively. The emergent fine-structure constant is
large~\cite{Pace2021}. A field wave cannot be made small in amplitude,
only rearranged in position---which is a statement about
short-wavelength physics, precisely the regime the Gaussian expansion
does not control.

Existing numerics stop short of that regime in a specific sense.
Gaussian and semiclassical treatments expand about small flux and are
controlled in the infrared, though they already predict substantial
anharmonic renormalization and an interaction-induced splitting of the
transverse polarizations~\cite{Benton2012,Kwasigroch2017,
Kwasigroch2020,Szabo2019}. Quantum Monte Carlo with analytic
continuation resolves broadened lineshapes and finds zone-boundary
weight beyond Gaussian electrodynamics, but not individual eigenstates
or their matrix elements~\cite{Huang2018}. Constrained-space exact
diagonalization has reached 96 spins, organized around ground-state
and low-energy electrodynamic parameters rather than the full
response~\cite{Pace2021}. What has not been done is the direct
question: at large $|\bq|$, \emph{which} eigenstates of the compact
theory carry the spin response, and how much.

We answer it. Our punchlines are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item In the Gaussian theory the two transverse modes are
\emph{exactly} degenerate at every momentum of our clusters, and any
renormalization of its two parameters rescales both equally. We
measure splittings up to a factor $1.69$ at the zone-boundary $L$
point, and they are not a function of $|\bq|$: two momenta at
identical $|\bq|$ split by $1.69$ and $1.06$. This is a qualitative
failure of the Gaussian description, not a quantitative correction to
it, and it is momentum-specific rather than a generic large-$|\bq|$
effect.
\item At $L$ the lower member does not merely split off; it collapses
to $0.50$ of the Gaussian energy and takes $50$--$54\%$ of the
longitudinal weight. At every other momentum we resolve it sits
between $0.82$ and $1.11$ of the Gaussian energy. The dominant
zone-boundary response is therefore not a photon.
\item This mode is elementary---the lowest excitation of its sector at
every size where the low-lying spectrum is available---and its
photon-like partner cannot be a two-mode composite, by spin-reversal
parity and by kinematics independently.
\item Its softness is quantitatively predicted, before any excited
state is computed, by the Feynman ratio $f_1(\bq)/S(\bq)$: this
ground-state quantity is a rigorous variational upper bound, and it
ranks all five resolved momenta in exactly the order of their measured
softening. The mechanism it exposes is an exact lattice cancellation
in the oscillator strength multiplied by a quantum enhancement of the
static correlations.
\item The reconstruction is stable across four clusters, three cluster
shapes, and a factor $1.6\times10^{4}$ in Hilbert-space dimension, and
sign-free Monte Carlo carries the ground-state diagnostic to $2048$
spins. It predicts a two-feature zone-boundary response whose energy
ratio and weight ratio are both independent of the overall energy
scale.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Model, observable, and method.}---We study the standard ring
Hamiltonian in the spin-ice manifold~\cite{Hermele2004,Shannon2012},
$H=-K\sum_p(W_p+W_p^\dagger)$ with
$W_p=S_1^+S_2^-S_3^+S_4^-S_5^+S_6^-$ summed over contractible
elementary hexagons; in gauge language $W_p=e^{iB_p}$ and $H$ is the
compact magnetic energy $-2K\sum_p\cos B_p$. Because $S^z$ is the
emergent electric field, the natural probe is the sublattice-summed
longitudinal response
$\Szz(\bq,\omega)=N^{-1}\sum_{\mu,n}|\langle
n|A^\mu_{\bq}|0\rangle|^2\delta(\omega-\omega_n)$ with
$A^\mu_{\bq}=\sum_{i\in\mu}e^{-i\bq\cdot\bm r_i}S^z_i$. All spectral
fractions below are normalized within $\Szz$ at fixed $\bq$. This is
the theoretical pseudospin response, not a material-specific neutron
cross section, which additionally involves coherent sublattice
interference, the local easy axes, the $g$ tensor, form factors, and
the polarization projector; we treat that fully in the Supplemental
Material.

We evaluate $\Szz$ by complete diagonalization at 48 sites and by
Lanczos continued fractions at 64, 72, and 96, the last in a winding
sector of dimension $146\,758\,352$. Winding sectors are certified
exhaustively at 48 and 72 sites---all $155$ and $310$ sectors
diagonalized, margins $0.99K$---and by the twelve largest of $519$ at
96, where the margin is $0.375K$. Recomputing the 96-site spectra at
Krylov dimension $200$ rather than $120$ leaves the dominant pole
energies and residues unchanged to machine precision. Details are in
the Supplemental Material.

\emph{The Gaussian doublet and what replaces it.}---The Gaussian
theory of this model is lattice electrodynamics with two parameters,
an electric stiffness $U$ and the ring coupling $K$. Diagonalizing the
lattice curl at momentum $\bq$ gives its transverse spectrum,
$\omega_\lambda(\bq)=\sqrt{2UK}\,\sigma_\lambda(\bq)$, with
$\sigma_\lambda$ the nonzero singular values of the curl. On every
cluster and at every momentum we examined, exactly two are nonzero and
they are \emph{equal} to machine precision: $\sigma_1=\sigma_2$, e.g.\
$2\sqrt3$ at $L$ and $4$ at $X$. The two transverse polarizations are
degenerate throughout the zone, and because $U$ and $K$ enter only
through the prefactor, that degeneracy survives \emph{any}
renormalization of the Gaussian theory, however strong. A splitting of
the doublet cannot be produced within that description at all.

Figure~\ref{fig:dispersion} shows what the exact spectrum does
instead. At the smallest momentum we resolve, the two dominant levels
are nearly degenerate---their ratio is $0.957$---and both sit within
$12\%$ of the Gaussian energy, obtained by fixing the single scale
$\sqrt{2UK}$ from the long-wavelength spectrum. This is a weakly
renormalized photon doublet, as expected. At the remaining momenta the
doublet splits, by factors from $1.06$ to $1.69$.

The splitting is momentum-specific, not a generic consequence of large
$|\bq|$, and the cluster provides its own control. At $|\bq|=0.866$
exactly, $L$ splits by $1.69$ while $(\frac16,\frac16,\frac56)$ splits
by only $1.06$; at $|\bq|=1$ exactly, $X$ splits by $1.53$ and
$(\frac13,\frac13,\frac23)$ by $1.19$. Identical wavevector magnitude,
splittings differing by up to a factor of $1.6$: whatever produces the
effect selects particular momenta, not a shell.

Splitting alone, however, is still not the phenomenon. At $X$ the
doublet splits substantially yet its lower member sits at $0.952$ of
the Gaussian energy---split, but nothing has softened. At $L$ both
happen at once: the splitting is largest \emph{and} the lower member
falls to $0.503$ of the Gaussian energy, half. It is that combination,
not the splitting by itself, that we call the reconstruction, and $L$
is the only momentum in Table~\ref{tab:momenta} where it occurs. The
softening is likewise not ordered by $|\bq|$---the same two momenta at
$|\bq|=0.866$ give $0.503$ and $1.114$.

\emph{The mode is elementary.}---Before interpreting it we establish
what it is not. At 48, 64, and 72 sites, where the low-lying spectrum
is available in full, the reconstructed level is the \emph{lowest
excitation of its sector}---$1.024K$, $0.954K$, $1.068K$, with nothing
between it and the ground state. It cannot be a composite of anything.

Its photon-like partner cannot be a two-mode composite either, and two
independent arguments exclude it. Global spin reversal $S^z\to-S^z$ is
a symmetry of $H$; the ground state is even and $A_{\bq}$ is odd, so
only odd states carry weight in $\Szz$. Any state built from an even
number of elementary excitations is even and therefore exactly dark to
this probe---we verify the complementarity numerically to $10^{-25}$.
The upper branch carries $22$--$32\%$ of the weight, so it is odd.
Independently, kinematics: two elementary excitations cost at least
$2\times1.024K=2.047K$ at 48 sites and three cost $3.07K$, while the
upper branch lies at $1.730K$, below the two-particle threshold. Both
branches are single elementary excitations.

\emph{Why $L$: the oscillator strength.}---The location of the
reconstruction is not an accident of the cluster, and it can be
derived. The two lowest moments of $\Szz$ are ground-state quantities.
The zeroth is the equal-time structure factor,
$m_0=\int\!d\omega\,\Szz=\langle A^\dagger_{\bq} A_{\bq}\rangle\equiv
S(\bq)$, which measures how much static correlation at wavevector
$\bq$ the ground state already holds. The first follows from the
$f$-sum rule,
\begin{equation}
m_1=\int\!d\omega\,\omega\,\Szz(\bq,\omega)
=\tfrac12\bigl\langle\bigl[A^\dagger_{\bq},[H,A_{\bq}]\bigr]\bigr\rangle .
\label{eq:fsum}
\end{equation}
For the ring Hamiltonian this evaluates in closed form. Since $W_p$
raises $S^z$ on alternating sites of its hexagon and lowers it on the
others, $[A_{\bq},W_p]=g_p(\bq)W_p$ with
$g_p(\bq)=\sum_{j\in p}(-1)^{j}e^{-i\bq\cdot\bm r_j}$, the
\emph{alternating lattice curl} of the imposed field wave around $p$.
Then $[H,A_{\bq}]=K\sum_p g_p(W_p-W_p^\dagger)$, and
Eq.~(\ref{eq:fsum}) collapses to
\begin{equation}
f_1(\bq)\equiv m_1=\frac{K}{2N}\sum_p
\bigl\langle W_p+W_p^\dagger\bigr\rangle
\sum_\mu\bigl|g^\mu_p(\bq)\bigr|^2 .
\label{eq:f1}
\end{equation}
This has the form of a Maxwell magnetic energy and reads directly: the
cost of imposing an electric-field wave of wavevector $\bq$ is the
squared curl that wave drives through each plaquette, weighted by how
strongly that plaquette actually resonates in the ground state. It
contains no fitted parameter and no excited state.

Their ratio,
\begin{equation}
\omega_F(\bq)=\frac{f_1(\bq)}{S(\bq)}
=\frac{\langle 0|A^\dagger_{\bq}(H-E_0)A_{\bq}|0\rangle}
{\langle 0|A^\dagger_{\bq} A_{\bq}|0\rangle},
\label{eq:sma}
\end{equation}
is simultaneously two things. Trivially it is the centroid of
$\Szz(\bq,\omega)$, first moment over zeroth. More usefully, the
right-hand side of Eq.~(\ref{eq:sma}) is the expectation value of
$H-E_0$ in the normalized single-mode state
$A_{\bq}|0\rangle/\sqrt{S(\bq)}$, which is orthogonal to the ground
state for $\bq\ne0$. Hence $\omega_F$ is a \emph{rigorous variational
upper bound} on the lowest odd excitation at $\bq$. This is Feynman's
construction for the helium roton~\cite{Feynman1954}, and it is why
the quantity is worth computing: a small $\omega_F$ does not describe
a soft mode after the fact, it \emph{certifies} from ground-state data
alone that one exists.

It does more than certify. Normalized to the Gaussian energy so that
momenta of different $|\bq|$ are comparable, $\omega_F/\omega_G$ ranks
all five resolved momenta in exactly the order of their measured
softening (Table~\ref{tab:momenta}), with no inversions: $L$ is
predicted softest and is softest, $(\frac13,\frac13,\frac23)$ second
and is second, and the three momenta the predictor places together at
$1.37$--$1.42$ are the three that do not soften at all. Since neither
the softening nor the splitting is ordered by $|\bq|$, this is not a
trivial correlation: on the shell $|\bq|=0.866$ the predictor assigns
$0.908$ to $L$ and $1.415$ to $(\frac16,\frac16,\frac56)$, and the
measured softenings are $0.503$ and $1.114$. The bound is not
tight---$\omega_F(L)=1.846K$ against a true $1.024K$, since the
centroid also carries the high-energy weight---but as an ordering of
which momenta reconstruct it is exact on this data.

Equation~(\ref{eq:f1}) also says why $L$ wins, and it takes both
factors. Geometrically, for $\bq\parallel[111]$ one of the four
hexagon families lies entirely within the wavefronts of the field
oscillation, so its alternating curl vanishes identically: the
family-resolved geometric weights are $(96,96,0,96)$ at $L$ against
$(96,96,96,96)$ at $X$. A quarter of the plaquettes cost nothing and
$f_1$ drops. Geometry alone is not enough---the momentum
$(\frac13,\frac13,\frac13)$ has a family cancellation too,
$(72,0,72,72)$, and does not soften, because its $S$ is the smallest
in the table. The second ingredient is dynamical: $S(L)=0.370$ is the
largest, while the equal-amplitude superposition over the same ice
configurations gives only $0.297$ and classical loop Monte Carlo on a
2048-site manifold is flat among zone-boundary momenta. The
enhancement is made by the interacting quantum amplitudes, not by the
ice constraint---which is what distinguishes this from a
Rokhsar--Kivelson resonon, whose softening follows from classical
correlations. $L$ is the one momentum where a vanishing curl family
coincides with the strongest static correlations, and
Eq.~(\ref{eq:sma}) multiplies the two.

\emph{Interpretation: this is a roton.}---We use the term in Feynman's
specific sense---the collective mode created by the density-wave
operator at finite momentum, soft because $f_1/S$ has a minimum
there---and not as a synonym for ``low-lying mode.'' Four measured
statements identify the reconstructed branch as that object, none of
which refer to it as ``the lower of two levels.''

First, it is what the density-wave operator makes: the single-mode
state $A_{\bq_L}|0\rangle$ overlaps the reconstructed multiplet with
squared amplitude $0.88$, $0.87$, and $0.86$ at 48, 64, and 72 sites,
coupling to its partner only through the orthogonal transverse
channel. The Feynman construction produces this branch and not the
other; that, rather than an energy ordering, is the categorization.
Second, it is elementary, as established above. Third, its softness is
predicted by $\omega_F$ in rank order across momenta. Fourth, relaxing
the single-mode state within the exact spectrum recovers $28$--$32\%$
of its energy, a size-stable amount, through a strongly
family-selective reorganization of ring coherence---the correlated
relaxation that in helium carries Feynman's bound down toward the true
roton.

The lattice modifies the geometry of the phenomenon and we state that
plainly. Along the radial $[111]$ direction the centroid rises from
$\Gamma$ to $L$ and folds back by periodicity, so there is no
helium-like radial minimum; the minimum lives in the zone-boundary
manifold, and its shallow extension along $(t,\frac12,\frac12)$ is why
the 72-site cluster finds a second reconstructed momentum on that
line.

\emph{What the mode does microscopically.}---One further property uses
no fitted scale and no Gaussian comparison at all. Adding the
Rokhsar--Kivelson term $H(V)=H+V\sum_p n_p^{\rm flip}$, with
$n_p^{\rm flip}$ the projector onto a flippable alternating hexagon,
and applying Hellmann--Feynman at the physical point $V=0$ gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\omega_n}{dV}\Big|_{V=0}=
\Bigl\langle n\Bigl|\sum_p n_p^{\rm flip}\Bigr|n\Bigr\rangle-
\Bigl\langle 0\Bigl|\sum_p n_p^{\rm flip}\Bigr|0\Bigr\rangle,
\label{eq:census}
\end{equation}
an exact state-resolved census of the flippable hexagons an excitation
adds or removes. Flippability is the resource that ring resonance
consumes: a hexagon must be flippable to contribute to the energy at
all. The ground state balances having many flippable plaquettes
against phasing them coherently, and a typical excitation loses on
both counts---averaged over a sector the census is exactly
$\mathrm{nnz}(H)/D-\langle n^{\rm flip}\rangle_0<0$, and at 48 sites
only $58$ of $9104$ eigenstates are positive.

The reconstructed mode is one of them, at every size and every
momentum where the reconstruction occurs: $+0.339$, $+0.338$,
$+0.134$, $+0.152$, $+0.259$. It is resource-rich and
coherence-poor---it rearranges spins so that \emph{more} hexagons
could flip while the ring processes on them are less coherent, paying
its energy in resonance rather than in the ice rule. Conversely, every
dominant level at a momentum that does \emph{not} reconstruct loses
flippability: the inequivalent $L$ classes at identical $|\bq|$ on the
anisotropic 64- and 96-site clusters give $-0.166$ and $-0.015$,
$-0.088$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:rk}).

We are explicit about the limit of this diagnostic, because it bears
on how much weight the interpretation can carry. The sign tracks the
\emph{momentum}, not the branch ordering: at a reconstructing momentum
the photon-like partner is negative at 48 and 64 sites but positive at
72 and 96, so a positive census is not by itself proof that a given
level is the roton. What is uniform across all our data is the pair of
statements just made---the reconstructed mode always gains, everything
at a nonreconstructing momentum always loses---and it is in that form
that the census enters the argument.

\emph{Robustness.}---The three cluster shapes $2{\times}2{\times}n$,
$2{\times}3{\times}3$, and $2{\times}3{\times}4$ sample different
boundary identifications and the reconstruction survives all of them
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder}, Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}). The most robust
measures need no fitted scale: the branch ratio is $0.55$--$0.59$
across a factor $1.6\times10^{4}$ in sector dimension, and the weight
fraction is $50$--$54\%$. Absolute energies drift by about ten
percent, as expected at these sizes, and the census magnitude drifts
more. Not everything is stable and we say which: the polarization
splitting at $X$ falls from $42\%$ at 48 sites to $6\%$ at 64, and we
draw no conclusion from it.

Because $\omega_F$ needs only the ground state, it can be pushed far
beyond exact diagonalization. The ring Hamiltonian is stoquastic in
the ice basis, permitting sign-free Green's-function Monte Carlo with
forward walking, benchmarked against the exact 48-site $S(L)$ to every
digit. On complete allowed momentum grids of $864$ and $2048$ spins
the ratio at $L$ is $2.142(10)$ and $2.179(19)$, against $2.660(10)$
and $2.713(21)$ at $X$ and $2.72$--$2.78$ at $W$, $K$, and $U$: an
$L$-centered minimum $20$--$25\%$ deep that drifts by $1.7\%$ between
the two sizes (Fig.~\ref{fig:thermo}). The diagnostic that predicts
the reconstruction at 48 spins still has its minimum at $L$ at $2048$.

\emph{Experimental implications.}---The signature is a two-feature
zone-boundary response where a renormalized photon gives one.
Following $[111]$ outward, a single dispersing mode at small $\bq$
separates into two, the splitting growing from unresolvable to a
factor $1.7$ by the zone boundary, with the \emph{lower} feature
carrying two to three times the weight of the upper. Two aspects are
scale-free and so testable without knowing $K$: the ratio of the two
energies, $0.55$--$0.59$, and the ratio of their weights. A single
photon branch with a renormalized velocity produces neither,
and---this is the sharper point---no Gaussian theory produces a
splitting at all, since its doublet is degenerate for every choice of
parameters. Because the reconstruction tracks $f_1/S$ rather than the
label $L$, the same signature should appear along
$(t,\frac12,\frac12)$, as it does in our 72-site spectrum.

Two caveats bound this. A material-specific neutron cross section
reweights the sublattice amplitudes coherently, so the theoretical
$50$--$54\%$ fraction is not a universal neutron intensity; and longer
ring exchanges, virtual spinons, and other microscopic terms can
modify the zone-boundary spectrum of a real material. The robust
prediction of the minimal model is the redistribution itself.

\emph{Discussion.}---The emergent photon of quantum spin ice is
nonperturbatively reconstructed at the lattice scale. Its transverse
doublet, exactly degenerate in the Gaussian theory for any parameters,
splits by a factor $1.7$ at the zone boundary, and the split-off
member falls to half the Gaussian energy while taking half the
longitudinal weight. That member is an elementary excitation; it is
the mode the electric-field density wave creates; its softness is
predicted in rank order by a ground-state bound whose two factors
identify the mechanism---an exact curl cancellation on one hexagon
family, multiplied by quantum-enhanced static correlations---and it is
microscopically distinguished by gaining ring flippability where
nonreconstructing momenta lose it. The phenomenology is Feynman's and
the object is a lattice roton.

What makes this possible is the structural point we began with: the
compact theory has no small parameter at short wavelength. Because
$E_\ell=\pm\frac12$ is saturated, a finite-amplitude field wave cannot
be built by increasing an amplitude, only by rearranging which bonds
carry which of the two allowed values---and that rearrangement is
cheapest where the curl vanishes and the static correlations are
strongest. A Gaussian photon has no such freedom, which is why no
renormalization of it reaches this mode.

Sharpness is established up to 96 spins; beyond that the Monte Carlo
constrains the bound, not the pole, and whether the level broadens
into a narrow continuum in the thermodynamic limit is open. The
natural next questions are whether the reconstruction survives virtual
spinons and longer ring exchanges in microscopic XXZ models, and
whether it can be seen directly---a question that requires dynamical
rather than ground-state Monte Carlo.
